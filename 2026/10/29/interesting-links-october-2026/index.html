Interesting Links

As 2026 hurtles towards the holiday season and then on into 2027, I took a moment to canvass opinion from some of y’all as to the shape of this newsletter.

For the last year I’ve included a section on AI, which has grown from a passing note through to a significant proportion of the newsletter (a third of it at times). So yesterday I asked on both Substack and LinkedIn whether I should split this out into its own post. I figured that some people might want only AI…and some people no AI.

The vote was relatively evenly split, but came down in favour of keeping it as is. So, that is what I shall do :)

As always, I’m very open to feedback on the newsletter - good and bad. Drop me an email, or use the comments section below.

Next week I’m looking forward to returning to San Francisco for Current 2026. If you’ll be there, come and say hi :) I’ll be at the unofficial run on Wednesday morning, and taking part in the Streamhouse AMA at midday in the meetup hub too.

  • 🔥 Linus Torvalds (for it is he) makes it very clear what he thinks of some of the absolutism around not using AI:

    I don’t understand this whole idiotic concept of "don’t use AI for X".

    Here’s a new rule just for you: don’t use compilers for any of your kernel submissions.

    Because if you can’t show that you really understand what the machine is actually doing by writing your code in assembler, then I don’t want to see your code contribution. You clearly aren’t competent if you can’t write it that way.

    […]

    So no. We’re not going to make stupid rules that say "you can’t use tools for X".

    The kind of expectations I have is that people do their best and I expect people to understand what they are doing.

    But that understanding has very little to do with what tools they use.
